The Detail Nobody Sees

Where the hours go on a hand-built website, and why visitors feel them even when they never see them.

By Marcus Vallance, Founder, On The Ground Productions · 3 min read · 2 November 2026

In short

  • Most of the work in a good website is detail visitors never consciously see.
  • It covers the design and motion, the hand-written code, speed, and the search detail on every page.
  • Every site is tested rigorously before launch and again after it goes live, with fresh eyes.
  • Done well, it adds up to a site that feels right.

Most of what goes into a good website, nobody notices.

People notice when it’s wrong. A page that takes a moment too long. Text that sits awkwardly on a phone. A button slightly out of line. Small things add up to a feeling that something’s off, even when people couldn’t tell you what it was.

It works the other way too. When every small part is right, a site just feels right. That takes hours, and most of them go into things you’ll never consciously see.

The design

Every page is designed in detail, with motion built in from the start. A site might open on a 3D version of the logo before giving way to a hero film. Services can move in the same carousel as the rest of the site, so the whole thing moves the same way.

Type, spacing and colour get the same attention. Big, confident type can set the tone for one business. For another, a quiet serif and a soft palette keep things calm, so the work does the talking.

The code underneath

The part nobody sees at all is the code. We write it by hand, so each page is clean, works on any screen size, and is easy for search engines to read.

Speed

Images, films and fonts are all prepared so pages load quickly on any connection, and every site is hosted on Cloudflare. A beautiful page that makes people wait has already lost some of them.

Search and AI search

Every page also carries detail that visitors never see: its title, its description, and the structured data that tells Google who you are and what you do. Each one is checked, page by page. How we plan for search across a whole site is its own post: SEO and AI search, built in from day one.

Testing, before and after launch

Every site goes through rigorous testing before it launches, and again once it’s live.

It’s very easy to get too close to the work. After weeks inside a site, you stop seeing it the way a first-time visitor does. So we step back and look at it from the outside, as someone who has never seen it before, and check that every route through the site is as clear as it can be.

That doesn’t stop at launch. Once a site is live, we keep checking that it’s as intuitive as possible, and refine it where it isn’t.

Knowing when to stop

Detail has a trap of its own. There’s always one more thing you could adjust. Part of the craft is knowing when a page is right, and leaving it alone.

Why it matters

People decide quickly what they think of a business from its website. All of this small, careful work is what makes that first impression a good one.

We couldn’t be happier with the new website Marcus built for us. His attention to detail is phenomenal, and the finished site is way beyond what we originally expected.

Neil Hudson, Director, Quantock Financial

Frequently asked questions

Why does detail matter on a website?

Visitors judge a business by how its site looks and feels. Small problems add up to a sense that something’s off, and small details done well add up to trust.

What goes into a website besides the design?

The code underneath, speed, and the search detail on every page: titles, descriptions and the structured data that tells Google who you are.

How do you test a website before launch?

Rigorously, and from the outside. We step back from the work and look at the site as a first-time visitor would, checking every route through it is clear. We keep testing after launch too.

Do you design for phones as well as desktop?

Yes. After the initial build, the desktop and phone versions of every site are each designed and detailed in their own right, so the experience is as good as it can possibly be on both.
