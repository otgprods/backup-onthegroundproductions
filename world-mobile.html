A story told where it actually happened.

World Mobile set out to connect the unconnected. To prove what that really meant, we didn’t build a slideshow: we went to Tanzania and filmed it, an isolated village coming online for the very first time.

Client
World Mobile
Sector
Decentralised telecoms
Location
Tanzania
Scope
Two documentary films
Who they are

Connectivity, owned by people.

World Mobile is building a different kind of phone network, one that people can own a piece of, powered by a sharing economy of community-run nodes rather than a handful of giant carriers. From the very beginning, its mission was simple and enormous: connect the unconnected, and close the digital divide for the billions of people still offline.

It’s exactly the kind of idea that’s easy to state and hard to feel. Which is why they came to us.

The brief

On the ground, not on a slide.

These films were made right at the start of World Mobile’s journey. With a token generation event on the horizon, they needed to tell their story, and tell it convincingly, to a global audience deciding whether to believe in the vision.

So we packed the kit and travelled to Tanzania to capture the moment first-hand: what it actually looks like when an isolated village is connected to the internet, and what changes the instant it is. No stock footage, no staging. Real people, real place, real story.

“If the promise is connecting the unconnected, you don’t explain it from a desk. You go, and you film it happening.”

Why we shot on location
The revisit

What changed, in their own lives.

We came back. The second film returns to the same community to see how things had moved on, how connectivity had quietly reshaped everyday life, opening up learning, work, healthcare and contact with the wider world.

It’s the payoff to the first film’s promise: not a projection of impact, but the real thing, revisited and recorded.

Where they are now

From a village to a network.

The story landed. World Mobile went on to raise significant funding in a remarkably short window, and the vision kept scaling. Today it has grown into a fast-moving, user-owned mobile network with token-based rewards and community-operated AirNodes, reaching across the United States and dozens of countries worldwide.

It started with a single village in Tanzania, and a decision to tell that story properly. See where World Mobile is today
