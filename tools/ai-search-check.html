Is your website ready for AI search?

See whether ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Copilot and Google’s AI can read your site, understand what you do and recommend you. Plus Google search and phone speed, with a plain-English list of what to fix.

Free, no sign-up. Takes about a minute. Works on any public website.

    What it checks

    Three scores, one list of fixes.

    The check reads your home page and up to eight more pages from your sitemap, the way a search crawler would, plus your robots.txt, sitemap and llms.txt. Then it scores the site out of 100 three ways and lists what to fix, most useful first.

    1. 01

      AI search

      Which AI crawlers your robots.txt lets in, whether your words are in the page or only drawn by JavaScript, your business facts in structured data, sameAs links, questions answered, reviews, dates and llms.txt.

    2. 02

      Google search

      HTTPS, page titles and descriptions, headings, canonicals, noindex, thin pages, alt text, share images, broken pages, sitemap and robots.txt, across every page checked.

    3. 03

      Phone speed

      Google’s own phone speed test, the engine behind PageSpeed Insights: how fast the main content shows, how long the page is frozen, how much it jumps while loading, and what’s slowing it down. Plus how fast your server answers.

    Why it matters

    People now ask AI who to hire.

    More and more people skip the list of links and ask an assistant instead: “who’s a good web designer near me?”, “which accountant should I use?”. The assistant searches the web, reads a handful of sites and names a few businesses. If it can’t read yours, you’re not in the answer.

    Getting into those answers isn’t magic. AI search is built on the same foundations as Google: crawlers that are allowed in, pages with real words on them, and clear facts about who you are. Many sites fail on the basics without knowing it, often because a security setting or a page builder quietly shuts the crawlers out.

    A good score doesn’t guarantee you’ll be recommended. Reviews, mentions on other sites and the quality of your content matter too. But a poor score means you’re very unlikely to be.

    FAQ

    Questions

    Is the AI search check really free?

    Yes. It’s free, there’s no sign-up, and you can check as many sites as you like, including your competitors’. We built it because most businesses have no idea whether AI assistants can read their website.

    What is AI search readiness?

    It’s how easily AI assistants such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Copilot and Google’s AI Overviews can read your website, understand who you are, and trust you enough to recommend you. It depends on whether their crawlers are allowed in, whether your words are in the page itself, and whether your business facts are spelled out in structured data.

    Why don't ChatGPT or Claude recommend my business?

    The usual reasons are that their search crawlers (OAI-SearchBot for ChatGPT, Claude-SearchBot for Claude) are blocked, that your site’s text only appears once JavaScript runs, or that nothing on the site states plainly what you do, where, and for whom. Reviews and mentions on other sites matter too. The check shows which of these apply to you.

    How is the score worked out?

    The AI search score adds up ten checks worth 100 points: crawler access (25), business facts in structured data (15), whether the home page can be listed, whether the words are in the HTML, links to your other profiles, real questions answered (10 each), and llms.txt, reviews, dates and a sitemap (5 each). The Google search score starts at 100 and loses points for each problem, weighted by how many pages it affects. Phone speed is Google's own Lighthouse mobile score, the same test as PageSpeed Insights.

    Which pages does it check?

    Your home page plus up to eight more from your sitemap, starting with the main sections. If there’s no sitemap it follows the links on your home page. It also reads robots.txt, your sitemap and llms.txt.

    Do you store my website or results?

    We keep a simple record of which websites are checked and their three scores, so we can see how the tool is used. Nothing about you is stored: no name, email or IP address. The check itself runs in your browser, and the share link simply runs it again.

    Should I block GPTBot and other AI training crawlers?

    That’s your choice, and blocking them doesn’t affect this score. Training crawlers such as GPTBot, ClaudeBot and Google-Extended collect content to train AI models. Search crawlers such as OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot and PerplexityBot fetch pages to answer questions and cite you, so keep those allowed if you want to be recommended.

    What is llms.txt?

    A short plain-text file at yoursite.com/llms.txt that sums up who you are, what you do and which pages matter most, written for AI tools. It’s a new, optional standard. It won’t make up for a weak site, but it’s quick to add.

    How is this different from a normal SEO checker?

    Most free SEO checkers were built for Google’s blue links. This one checks the things AI search depends on as well: which AI crawlers you let in, your business schema, sameAs links, questions answered and llms.txt. It also covers Google search and phone speed, because AI search builds on the same index.
